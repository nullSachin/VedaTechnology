Chole Masala

Chickpeas simmered slowly in a dark, tomato‑onion masala until the gravy clings to every bite. The kind of Sunday lunch that fills the whole house with the smell of toasted spice.

  • 20 minprep
  • 45 mincook
  • 4servings
  • Mediumspice

Ingredients

For the chickpeas

  • 1½ cups dried chickpeas, soaked overnight
  • 1 black tea bag (for colour)
  • 1 tsp salt

For the masala

  • 3 tbsp oil or ghee
  • 1 bay leaf, 2 green cardamom
  • 1 large onion, finely chopped
  • 1 tbsp ginger-garlic paste
  • 2 tomatoes, pureed
  • 2 tsp chole masala powder
  • 1 tsp Kashmiri red chilli powder
  • ½ tsp turmeric
  • 1 tsp roasted cumin powder
  • 1 tsp amchur (dried mango powder)

To finish

  • Sliced onion, green chilli, coriander leaves
  • Steamed rice or bhature, to serve

Preparation

  1. Cook the chickpeas

    Drain the soaked chickpeas and pressure‑cook with the tea bag, salt and 3 cups water for 5–6 whistles, until soft enough to mash between two fingers. Discard the tea bag; keep the cooking liquid.

  2. Build the base

    Heat oil in a heavy pot. Add the bay leaf and cardamom and let them sizzle for a few seconds, then add the onion and cook until deep golden — this is what gives the gravy its colour, so don't rush it.

  3. Add aromatics and spice

    Stir in the ginger-garlic paste until the raw smell fades, then add the tomato puree and cook until the oil separates at the edges. Add the chole masala, chilli powder and turmeric, and fry for a minute more.

  4. Simmer

    Tip in the cooked chickpeas along with their liquid. Bring to a boil, then partially cover and simmer for 20–25 minutes, mashing a few chickpeas against the side of the pot to thicken the gravy.

  5. Finish and serve

    Stir in the roasted cumin powder and amchur, and adjust salt. Top with raw onion, green chilli and coriander, and serve hot with rice or bhature.

Chef's note — the tea bag isn't traditionally essential, but it's what gives restaurant‑style chole its deep, almost black‑brown colour without extra spice. Leftovers taste even better the next day.
